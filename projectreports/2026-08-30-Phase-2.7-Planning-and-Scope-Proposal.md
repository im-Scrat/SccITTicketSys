## 1. Executive Summary

**Phase 2.7 does not need to be invented — the SPMP already defines it.**
WP-2.7 is *"Administration pt.1: notifications + preferences + announcements
(FR-NOT-*)"*, and WP-2.6b's closure explicitly carried its notification debt
into it. That is the single strongest piece of evidence in this exercise, and I
have built the proposal around it rather than around the deferred-work list.

Three findings shape the recommendation:

1. **The notification architecture is fully designed and entirely unbuilt.**
   SDD §26 and DD-52 specify a bespoke database channel driver, a preference
   gate and thirteen triggers. The `notifications` and `notification_preferences`
   tables exist and are empty; the models and enums exist; **five mail-only
   notifications exist for Identity flows**; there is **no channel driver, no
   notification service, and zero frontend notification code**. This is
   design-complete, build-zero work — the lowest-risk substantial package
   available.

2. **AI is Phase 3, not Phase 2.7.** Every `FR-AI-*` requirement is marked
   **P3**, and the SPMP places them in WP-3.1–3.5 gated behind *"P2 GA"*.
   Pulling AI into Phase 2.7 would contradict the approved plan. I recommend
   against it.

3. **The queue has never been exercised.** There are **zero `ShouldQueue`
   classes** in the codebase. The worker and Redis run, and two scheduled
   commands execute daily, but nothing is ever queued. Notifications and the
   digest will be the first real consumers — which makes queue reliability a
   Phase 2.7 concern rather than an assumption.

**Recommended Phase 2.7:** four work packages — notification infrastructure,
the notification experience, announcements, and a quality/operational-readiness
package that closes the verification gaps this session exposed. Settings,
branding, audit viewers, analytics, assets pt.3, AI and the floor plan stay out.

**Nine decisions are required from you before development can begin** (§24).

## 5. New Requirements Requiring Owner Confirmation

**I have not invented requirements.** These are areas where the evidence
suggests functionality will be wanted but the documents do not define it. Each
needs your decision before it could become a requirement.

### C1 — Notification digest storage location

- **Why relevant:** FR-NOT-008 references `notifications.digest_enabled`, but the
  live `notifications` table has columns `id, uuid, user_id, type, title,
  message, data, action_url, read_at, created_at` — **there is no
  `digest_enabled` column**. `notification_preferences` carries
  `user_id, channel, notification_type, is_enabled`.
- **Touches:** notification preferences, the digest job.
- **Roles:** all.
- **Value:** without resolution the digest has no opt-in switch.
- **Architecture/DB:** either a `users.digest_enabled` column, a reserved
  `notification_preferences` row, or an SRS correction. All three are cheap; they
  are not equivalent.
- **Security:** none.
- **Decision needed:** where the digest opt-in lives, and whether the SRS
  sentence is amended to match.

### C2 — Notification retention and volume

- **Why relevant:** thirteen triggers on a school-wide system with no stated
  retention. `notifications` grows unbounded.
- **Touches:** notification centre performance, backups, the unread badge.
- **Value:** avoids a table that must be fixed under load later.
- **DB:** a retention/pruning policy, possibly a scheduled prune command.
- **Decision needed:** retain forever, prune read items after N days, or archive.

### C3 — Announcement authorship and lifecycle

- **Why relevant:** FR-NOT-010 defines audience, window, active and pin flags —
  but not whether announcements notify, who may edit another's announcement, or
  what happens at expiry.
- **Touches:** notification triggers, dashboards, audit.
- **Roles:** administrator authors; all read.
- **Decision needed:** does publishing an announcement also raise a
  notification, and is expiry a delete or a state?

### C4 — Escalation and SLA behaviour once notifications exist

- **Why relevant:** SLA breach is a named trigger (FR-NOT-003), and **OI-06
  (auto-escalation) is still open**. Notifications make breach visible for the
  first time, which usually prompts "and then what?"
- **Touches:** Tickets lifecycle, assignment, notifications.
- **Value:** high, but it changes workflow semantics.
- **Decision needed:** resolve OI-06 — notify only, or act.

### C5 — Who receives what

- **Why relevant:** FR-NOT-003 lists *events*, not *recipients*. "Ticket status
  change" could reach the reporter, the assignee, all administrators, or
  followers — and there is no follower concept in the schema.
- **Touches:** every trigger; row-scoped visibility must not be leaked *through*
  a notification.
- **Security:** **a notification is a disclosure channel.** A message naming a
  machine or a technician can reveal what the recipient's own visibility rules
  would hide. This needs explicit rules, not defaults.
- **Decision needed:** a recipient matrix per trigger.

### C6 — Operational data onboarding

- **Why relevant:** the production-target database now holds **one user and no
  operational data**. Before any real use, someone must create the estate —
  buildings, rooms, PC units, assets, users.
- **Touches:** Locations, Assets, Identity.
- **Value:** without it the system cannot be used, however complete the features.
- **Decision needed:** bulk import (part of WP-2.4b's import/export), manual
  entry, or a seeding exercise — and when.

| **Reporting/Analytics** | Dashboard only. Untouched by Phase 2.7 |
| **AI/RAG** | pgvector and 10 `ai_*` tables baselined and empty. Untouched by Phase 2.7 |

| **Production deployment** | The weakest area — no tooling. Addressed by 2.7d as tooling, not architecture |

## 13. Security Impact

| Area | Phase 2.7 impact |
|---|---|
| **Disclosure through notifications** | **The principal new risk.** WP-2.6b built careful row-scoped visibility; a notification pushes content *outward* and can bypass it. | **Queue security** | Payloads persist in Redis. Serialized notifications should carry identifiers, not sensitive content |
| **CSP** | 2.7d(iv) removes a fragile hand-maintained hash — a security-availability improvement |
| **Deployment tooling** | 2.7d(iii) reduces the chance of operating on the wrong stack |
| **Unchanged** | Authentication, Sanctum, rate limiting, attachment boundary, MFA (OI-03, still deferred) |
